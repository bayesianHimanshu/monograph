**Reading across models.**

* Every arm answered at least 0.97 of the multi-hop and identity questions (T2+T3+T4) with both models. The questions did not separate the arms, so this experiment cannot show the graph store winning; it shows that with a well-described schema, both stores and both interfaces reach the answers. The differences that remain are a few questions each and do not reach the 0.10 margin the PREREG requires.
* Identity questions (T3) scored at least 1.00 on every arm. The identity layer did the work: the crosswalks, moiety links, generic links and aliases are data that both stores carry, as tables and as edges, so text-to-SQL resolved an 11-digit NDC, an RxCUI, a salt form or a firm alias as reliably as the graph tools. What made identity answerable was modeling identity, not storing it as a graph.
* The clearest gap is on aggregates (T5) for claude-haiku-5-5: S-free scored 0.88, its tools arms S-tools 1.00, G-tools 0.97. Of its 5 misses, 4 submitted the right set of ids (as many as the gold count) instead of the number: an answer-format failure that the PREREG scores as wrong, not a counting mistake. A `count` tool returns a number, so the tools arms rarely made it. That is an interface effect, on either store, and a small one.
* AGE's Cypher dialect cost the G-free agents failed calls (relationship-type alternation, reusing a bound variable in MATCH, internal errors on lists of vertices), but they recovered within the budget. A graph store did not make free-form querying easier here; it made it differently hard.
